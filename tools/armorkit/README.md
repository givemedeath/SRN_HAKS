# Armor Toolkit (tools/armorkit)

## Overview
The `tools/armorkit` package is the foundation for modular clothing, armor, headgear and accessory parts on the 20 SRN bodies: a part registry, the generator for the twelve `parts_*.2da` tables, the measured seam contract and head envelopes, and an isolated client fixture builder. It follows [the armorkit plan](../../docs/armorkit/plan.md); evidence for engine behaviour is in [the spike record](../../docs/armorkit/spikes.md) and the measurements are explained in [the seam contract](../../docs/armorkit/seam-contract.md). Carrying a generated outfit onto a body, splitting it into parts and writing them as compiled models are here; generation, materials and publication are later phases and are not here yet. Nothing in this package writes into a pack.

## Interface
- `[__main__.py:main]` - Command line: `registry` (validate, write the SR_NWN handoff file), `tables` (generate and check the parts tables; nothing is written unless all twelve pass), `measure` (seam contract and head envelopes from pack folders; `--only seams` or `--only heads` for one of them), `bake` (a generated outfit into the parts a body wears, with the gates that can be checked on it; below), `fixture` (build a client fixture; `--run` also starts the game on it, with `--timeout` and `--settle`).
- `[registry.py:validate]` / `[registry.py:load]` - Check a registry document (`srn-armorkit-registry`, schema version 1) and return its parts ordered by slot and number; `publish=True` also requires all 20 bodies.
- `[registry.py:SLOTS]` - Per slot: parts table, model tokens, the `xArmorPart_*` item fields and `ITEM_APPR_ARMOR_MODEL_*` indices.
- `[registry.py:model_name]` / `[registry.py:model_names]` / `[registry.py:texture_names]` - Resource names a part implies: `p<m|f><race><phenotype>_<slot><number>`.
- `[registry.py:block]` - The block a number belongs to: 300-999 `test`, 1000-2999 `standalone`, then the family of its thousand (3000s civilian, 4000s workwear, 5000s corporate, 6000s security, 7000s gang, 8000s heavy, 9000s runner).
- `[registry.py:handoff]` - The registry with derived table, item-field, model and texture names, as SR_NWN consumes it. A part's `badge` says where it shows a logo. The shared logo material, its parameter and the logo cells are not in the handoff yet (plan.md, revision "logos use the shader value"); that entry is Phase 1 work.
- `[tables.py:generate]` / `[tables.py:generate_all]` - Table text from stock text and registry parts: stock rows kept, gaps padded, live rows nulled.
- `[tables.py:check]` - Fails on a gap, a duplicate, a changed stock row or column row, a registry part without a live row or inside the stock range, or a live row no part owns.
- `[seams.py:JOINTS]` / `[seams.py:sides]` - The eight joints: over slot, under slot and the bones that define each plane; limb joints are recorded on sides `l` and `r`, the neck and waist on `c`.
- `[seams.py:load_contract]` / `[seams.py:envelope]` / `[seams.py:overlap]` / `[seams.py:mixes]` - Read the frozen `seam-contract.json`, get a tier's per-direction envelope at a joint (a limb joint needs its side) and its minimum overlap, and test whether two parts mix cleanly there.
- `[seams.py:section]` / `[seams.py:ring]` / `[seams.py:swing]` / `[seams.py:minimum_overlap]` - Plane sections, radial rings, joint swing and the overlap rule the contract is built from. `[seams.py:standoff]` is how far a section stands off the body ring per direction, farthest and nearest, the ring read straight from one direction's radius to the next (`[seams.py:around]`): what the seam gate holds an end to. `[seams.py:surface_points]` samples a surface no sparser than a step.
- `[measure.py:seam_contract]` / `[measure.py:head_envelopes]` - Rebuild the two data files from `srn_body`, `srn_head` and extracted stock animation models.
- `[measure.py:clearance]` - How far a worn part's ring stands outside the body ring at a joint. It refuses a torso piece at the neck, shoulder and hip, which a plane section cannot measure.
- `[measure.py:seat]` - How far the body's pelvis runs past a hip plane down its thigh, per 64th of a turn round the joint, evened over five: 6.8 to 8.2 cm on the Human male, 5.7 to 6.5 cm on the female. The line an outfit's hips are cut on (`[split.py:seated]`).
- `[measure.py:armhole]` - How far past a shoulder plane, down the arm, the body's upper arm comes out of its chest, per 64th of a turn round the joint, evened over five: the arm's skin shows where it lies outside the chest (`[measure.py:winding]`, the closed surface's winding number). Over the shoulder it lies up to 6.1 cm before the plane on the Human male (2.6 cm on the female), and at the armpit, where the arm hangs inside the torso's side, up to 14.8 cm past it (17.5 cm). The line an outfit's upper arms are cut on (`[split.py:armholed]`); 15 to 17 seconds a side.
- `[client_fixture.py:build]` - Write a fresh user directory holding a fixture hak, the packs under test and a module with scripted actors and camera shots. Actors wear bare skin parts unless given part numbers, colours or an item blueprint written with `xArmorPart_*` fields.
- `[client.py:run]` - Start the game on a built fixture, capture the window after each shot line, stop that client and return log lines and captures. A capture that would be taken after a later shot began, and a shot whose actor does not exist, fail the run.
- `[toolchain.py:cleanmodels]` - The standalone cleanmodels 4.17.5 executable pinned in `tools/shared-tools.lock.json`, resolved from the shared tool root with its bytes verified; `root` overrides the root. Armorkit compiles parts with it and takes its race versions only where they pass review.
- `[repose.py:onto_body]` - Carry a generated outfit onto a body: find the outfit's own joints from horizontal sections, bend each of its bones onto the body's and widen it about each bone until it stands outside the body. `[repose.py:load]` reads a generated mesh file into the bodies' axes; `[repose.py:outfit_joints]`, `[repose.py:repose]` and `[repose.py:fit]` are the three steps.
- `[split.py:split]` - Cut one outfit mesh into the rigid armor parts on the joint planes, given the joints in the mesh's space, which has the bodies' axes, and a radius per joint. Returns `{token: (vertices, faces, uvs)}`; with `overlaps`, each under part also gets a rounded cap past its joint plane, under the over part. `reaches` lengthens a plane's cut at a joint where the outfit is bulkier than its tier (a boot at the ankle, trousers at the hip), without changing the thickness that tells the parts apart; `[split.py:reaches]` measures them on the outfit. `whole` leaves a neck, wrist or ankle uncut, so an outfit without a neck part keeps its collar on the chest. `[split.py:assign]` returns the cut mesh with the part and the source face of every face. `made` collects what made each face of each part: the outfit's own surface, a closing, a cap, a thigh's wall toward the other leg, a cap's faces grown from a pad's rim, or the pelvis's strip between the legs; `origins` collects the face of the outfit each face of a part was cut from, -1 for what the split made. `shape` reshapes each part after it is cut and closed and before its caps grow, given its vertices, faces, the columns carried on its vertices and the outfit face each face came from (`[ends.py:shaper]` makes it); `deep` names the joints whose caps run the whole overlap deep from every rim point (`[split.py:cap]`, `bounded`). `seat` cuts the hips on the body's own seat line instead of the planes: the pelvis takes what the body's pelvis covers (`[split.py:seated]`) and is closed across each leg hole there (`[split.py:lid]`, `on`), and each thigh's end grows from its rim on the line (`[split.py:seat_end]`). `arms` does the same at the shoulders on the body's armhole: the chest takes what of the upper arm lies before it (`[split.py:armholed]`) and is closed across each armhole, and each upper arm gets one end grown from its whole opening there, in place of the closings along the sleeve's cuts and the cap. `[split.py:cut]` with `height` cuts along a value per vertex instead of a plane.
- `[texture.py:colour_map]` - Bake a part's texture from the generated outfit it was cut from: lay the part out (`[texture.py:layout]`, with the unwrapping library the caller loads), trace every texel back to the point of the generated mesh it came from and read the outfit's colour there (`[texture.py:open_field]` and `[texture.py:read_field]` read a saved colour field). `[texture.py:materials]` sorts the colours of the whole outfit into its materials and `[texture.py:tint_maps]` turns a part's colours into the shades and channel layers of its tint texture.
- `[texture.py:normal_map]` - Bake a part's normal map from the generator's own high-detail mesh, whose relief the simplified outfit lost: `[texture.py:normal_field]` gathers that mesh's surface directions per voxel, and a texel traced back as for the colours takes the fine surface's normal there, written in the simplified surface's own frame. `[texture.py:write_relief]` writes the map as `<model>n.tga` with the material file that binds it. The faces the split made stay flat, and the caller names them (`added`): told only by being smaller on the generated mesh than worn, a flat closing, which spans its opening from rim to rim, was given the relief of whatever lies inside the limb there, and showed as a patch of odd facets at a lifted shoulder and between the thighs.
- `[texture.py:relief_shade]` - The factor a part's normal map changes its shades by, so that relief shows in the game's even light: lighter on raised edges, darker in creases and on surfaces tilted away. `[texture.py:tint_maps]` takes the normal map as `relief` and the part's `[texture.py:chart_map]` as `charts`.
- `[export.py:write_part]` - Write one part as an ASCII model in its bone's space, with its normals, and its tint texture, both named like the model. `[export.py:compile_models]` compiles such models with the pinned cleanmodels, 40 to a call, into another folder, copies their tint textures along, and fails on any model that does not come out with its source's faces. `[export.py:vertex_normals]` gives the normals to take on an outfit before it is split: one per position across a texture seam, one each for two sheets lying back to back.

- `[bake.py:run]` - Bake one generated outfit into worn parts: `python -m tools.armorkit bake BRIEF OUTPUT --number N --body-pack srn_body --mesh EXPORTED.glb --colours FIELD.npz --raw GENERATED.glb --xatlas FOLDER [--kept FOLDER] [--pack srn_body --pack srn_armor] [--nwn-root GAME]`. `[bake.py:bake]` runs the stages in order (materials and trims on the mesh, `repose.onto_body`, pads, `split.split` with the hips cut on the body's seat line, the upper arms on its armhole and the ends shaped to the seam contract, layout, colours, relief), `[bake.py:tints]` finds the materials over the whole outfit and draws every part's tint texture, `[bake.py:write]` writes the models as ASCII with their tint textures, half-size normal maps and material files, and `--nwn-root` compiles them. Reading the colours and relief takes minutes; `--body-textures srn_body_tex` is needed for a brief that shows the body's skin. `--kept` stores the baked parts with a list of them and of the slots the brief leaves to the body or shares with its skin (`baked.json`), and `bake BRIEF OUTPUT --number N --body-pack srn_body --kept FOLDER` alone tints and writes them again: the listed parts, whatever else an earlier bake left in the folder. Only the models of the run are compiled. The record is `OUTPUT/bake.json`; the command exits 1 when a gate fails.
- `[bake.py:roles]` - What each face the split made is drawn as, by the part it is on: a closing, a cap, a pad's cap, or shaded as the cloth round it (a thigh's cap and wall, the pelvis's lids), and whose closings lie in an armhole's shadow (the upper arms'). The texture and export functions only say what their caller flags; this is the caller, and the one place those choices are made.
- `[bake.py:gates]` - The automatic gates of the plan that can be checked on one written outfit: triangle budgets (chest 6,000, another part 2,250, the outfit 30,000), textures of at most 1,024 texels a side, no model name a pack already holds, and texture closure (tint texture, material file and the map it names). Its report lists under `notBuilt` the plan's gates it does not check: clearance over the body, and holes or the seam contract where it cannot check them. A pass is not the plan's until those exist.
- `[bake.py:surface]` - The gate on holes and detached pieces, on a fresh bake: the generated outfit as the bake is given it, at the body's size and before it is cut, fails on an opening 0.5 cm round or more and on a separate piece under 10 cm² that stands more than 2 mm from the rest. Every opening the cut leaves is made on purpose (an over part is open on its joint plane and the under part stands in it), so the outfit is measured whole. A separate piece is not a fault in itself: knee pads 0.6 cm off the trousers, the gloves beyond a bare forearm. Measured on the Site Guard's seven exports, the two worn last pass, one closed surface; the first two had openings up to 9 cm round and 26 and 49 pieces, and in four of the seven a crumb of 2 to 6 cm² floated, three times in one place by a thigh. The record holds what was found as `surface`. Parts tinted again from a kept folder come without the outfit, and the gate stays under `notBuilt` there.
- `[bake.py:ends]` - The seam-contract gate, on every bake: each end the outfit has at a joint is held to the outfit's tier (docs/armorkit/seam-contract.md). An under end's section at the plane must lie inside the tier's envelope in all 16 directions, the envelope read straight from one direction's radius to the next (`[seams.py:around]`), and in each direction it is found in the end must run past the plane, into the over part, by the tier's minimum overlap; a point within 1.5 cm of the joint's axis, a cap's pole, counts for every direction. An over end's section 0.5 cm short of the plane must stand at least as far out as the envelope wherever it is found, which also tells it is open. The chest at the neck and shoulders and the pelvis at the hips are not measured: the plane cuts the torso wall there. A part that does not reach its joint fails, unless the brief shares its slot with the body's skin, and the body's skin beside the outfit's faces is never measured. A pad's faces (a guard over an elbow or a knee and the cap grown from its rim, `pad` in the kept parts) are left out of both sections, as the user chose: a pad stands proud of the limb by design. They count toward how far an end runs past the plane, since a pad's cap is what covers the hinge. The record holds per joint and side the tier each end meets, how far the under end stands outside the envelope and runs past the plane, and how far the over end stands inside it, as `seams`. A body the contract has no rings of (all but the two Human Fit bodies) leaves the gate under `notBuilt`. Cut as it came, the Site Guard failed at every joint at tier 2: its under ends stood up to 2.2 cm (elbows) to 8.9 cm (shoulders) outside the envelope and its over ends 1.3 to 3.2 cm inside it. With its ends shaped (`[ends.py:shaper]`) both ends at the elbows, wrists, waist, knees and ankles meet tier 2, the under ends 0.4 to 2 mm inside the envelope and the over ends 0.8 to 2.8 mm outside it. Cut on the body's seat line (`[split.py:seated]`), its thighs meet tier 2 at the hips, 0.2 and 0.4 mm inside the envelope and 12.4 cm past the plane of 11.9; cut on the hip plane they stood 7.3 cm outside it. Cut on the body's armhole (`[split.py:armholed]`), its upper arms meet tier 2 at the shoulders, 1.1 and 1.6 cm inside the envelope and 9.6 cm past the plane of 9.1, and every end of the Site Guard meets tier 2; cut on the plane, the closings along the sleeve's cuts stood up to 8.9 cm outside it beside the shoulder pads, and the caps ran 6.6 to 7.9 cm past.
- `[ends.py:shaper]` - The hook `split` reshapes each part with (`shape`): every end the part has at an elbow, wrist, waist, knee or ankle is brought to the seam contract's envelope of the outfit's tier before the part's caps grow, so a cap grows from the shaped rim. `[ends.py:ring]` reads the end's radius per 64th of a turn just on its own side of the plane, `[ends.py:end]` moves it there: an under end 0.5 mm inside the envelope, an over end 0.5 mm outside it plus how far one edge of its ring cuts inside its two ends (`[ends.py:target]`, `[ends.py:spread]`), the envelope read straight between its 16 directions. The end is held so for 1.2 cm from the plane and blends back into the outfit's own surface over 12 cm, or half the part's run if shorter. An end grown out is scaled about the axis; an end drawn in has only what stands above the body ring pressed toward it, and a closing inside the body ring stays. A face crossing the plane moves whole, and nothing farther from the axis than 1.3 times the end moves. A pad (`pads`, as `bake` flags them) is not read into the ring and rides along. The normals carried on the part turn as its surface did. Caps at those joints run the overlap and 0.5 cm more from every rim point (`DEEPER`, `split` `deep`). The shoulders, hips and neck are not shaped.

### Subdirectories & Data Assets
- `examples/registry.json` - A small valid, publishable registry used by the tests.
- `seam-contract.json` - Seam contract version 2, measured on the c7 Human Fit bodies, with the hashes of the body and animation models it came from.
- `head-envelopes.json` - Outer bounds of the published heads per race and gender, in head-bone space, from surfaces sampled every 3 mm.

## Invariants
- `[registry.py:validate_part]` - Part numbers start at 300; stock rows 0-200, stock models to 255 and the 256-299 buffer are never reused. Numbers stop at 9999; the item field holds up to 65535, and the numbers from 10000 are kept closed for more runner and role sets. Every model name is at most 16 characters.
- `[registry.py:validate_part]` - A family number reads as family, group, set, variant by digit, and its part names its `set` and `group`; a standalone number (1000-2999) has neither, and a standalone neck part sits in the band of its category (headgear 1000-1499, back 1500-1999, neckwear 2000-2499, torso 2500-2999); test numbers (300-999) are refused with `publish=True`.
- `[registry.py:validate_part]` - A number in a family block carries that family's tag; the chest is always a separate male and female cut; a fitted part declares a tier 0-3 at each of its joints; logos sit only on shoulder parts and neck-slot lanyards; the skin channel is used exactly when skin is exposed.
- `[registry.py:validate]` - One part per slot and number; ten numbers never belong to two sets and a hundred never belongs to two groups.
- `[tables.py:generate]` - Stock rows are copied byte for byte (201 rows, 39 for `parts_robe`); live rows are `COSTMODIFIER 0`, `ACBONUS 0.00`; padding rows are `****` in every column; rows count from 0 with no gaps up to the highest part number.
- `[repose.py:landmarks]` - An outfit's joints are placed from four heights read off its sections: the floor, the crotch (the lowest section with a curve across the middle), the armpits (the lowest above it and above the hands with nothing beside that curve) and the neck. The outfit must stand upright with its legs apart and its arms clear of the torso, or the call fails. The arms may hang or be held out to the sides (an A-pose), which keeps a vest and a sleeve from being generated as one surface; arms held out end above the hips, so the sections between hips and hands have nothing beside the torso either, and the armpit is looked for above the lowest section that has. Heights map from the body's to the outfit's through floor, crotch and neck, so an outfit with short legs gets its knees where its own legs have them.
- `[repose.py:past]` - An arm may be bare between a short sleeve and a glove: the forearm there is the body's. The armpit is then above a stretch of sections with something beside the torso, where the torso's curve is wider above that stretch than in it: a sleeve that has joined the torso. A guard that stands off the shoulder is beside the torso too, joins nothing and leaves the armpit where it is (the first such outfit, the Site Guard with its forearms taken out and the ends closed, had its armpit put just above the gloves and its shoulders at its wrists, without a word). The glove is the first curve below the sleeve's hem that lies farther from the middle than the hem does, which a leg never does, and gives the wrist and the fingertips as on a whole arm. It lies 5% of the outfit's height or more below the hem: nearer, it is the rest of a whole arm that a thin section parted, and that arm is followed through as a whole one. The upper arm lies on the line through the middles of the sleeve's rings, its closed sections across the arm, the one nearest the line where a section has several (`[repose.py:rings]`, `[repose.py:through]`; two rings at least, or the sleeve is refused): the shoulder where that line reaches the shoulder's height, the elbow where the upper arm and the forearm on to the wrist are as long as the body's are to each other. Two other lines were tried. Level sections cut a hem aslant, their middles lay 6 cm toward the armpit and the shoulder placed from them 13 cm out. The line from the glove to the sleeve is a bent arm's chord, and a trough cut along it left the right sleeve on the torso. A sleeve that ends at 60% of the upper arm or less is nowhere a tube below the armpit and is refused, and so is a sleeve with no glove beyond it: nothing says where its wrist is, and an arm followed for less than 60% of the body's, shoulder to fingertips, has none. The shoulder of such an arm may lie just outside the outfit's surface, so `[split.py:sleeve]` passes over sections with no curve round the arm's line for the first quarter of the upper arm; taken for the arm's end, they left the sleeve on the torso. Measured on that outfit with sleeves to 70% and 85%, at the body's size, against the whole arm's joints: the shoulder within 1 cm across and 8 cm farther forward (the whole arm's is continued back along a bent arm), the wrist within 3.5 cm, the elbow 3.5 to 4.5 cm. The bake of the 70% one runs through and passes its gates; its sleeves sit a median 3 to 4 cm from where the whole arm's bake puts the same points (5 to 5.5 cm for the worst twentieth), its gloves 1.5 to 2 cm, its chest 0.4 cm, its legs nowhere else. In the client its sleeves ride on the upper arms and its gloves on the hands, standing, with the arms up and forward, sitting and talking; how well a sleeve sits is for a real short-sleeved outfit to show, since this one's are the stumps of long ones.
- `[bodyskin.py:worn]` - A worn part takes the place of the body's part in its slot, so the upper-arm part of a short-sleeved shirt is the sleeve and the bare arm below its hem. The brief says so before the image is made: `"bare": ["forearm"]` names the slots the outfit leaves to the body and `"skin": ["bicep"]` those it shares with the body's skin (`[brief.py:validate]`; only those two are built). A bare slot gets no part (`[bake.py:left_to_body]`): what the outfit has there, a glove's cuff past the wrist, is cut off on the joint's plane, and the glove ends in the cap the split gives it. In a shared slot the body's own part is added to the outfit's with its vertices, its authored normals and its texture coordinates, so it meets the body's forearm at the elbow as it did. Its textures are a tint texture, a normal map and a roughness map (`[bodyskin.py:body_part]`, from the folder `bake --body-textures` names). The part's textures are as large a side as the body's and as twice the outfit's, to 1,024 at most (`[bake.py:skinned]`): the outfit's lie in the quarter from 0 and the body's whole textures in the quarter up to 1, at half their size on an upper arm whatever size the sleeve was laid out at. The body's coordinates are halved and moved, never laid out again: a normal map is read in the frame its coordinates span. The tint keeps the body's shade on the skin layer there (`[bodyskin.py:tinted]`), the normal map is written whole and not halved, and the roughness is a map the material file binds as `texture3` with the constant at 0 (`[texture.py:write_relief]`, `<model>r.tga`): the skin's own, and 0.8 on the outfit's texels. The body's edge texels run on through the two quarters nothing lies in, so that no smaller copy of a map the client makes mixes anything into the skin's edge but skin. What the sleeve hides of the arm is left out, but for 4 cm under the hem (`[bodyskin.py:shown]`): whole, the two were over a part's budget. In each of 16 directions round the arm the outfit's surface, with the closings and walls the split made at the shoulder, is taken in pieces, parted where 1.5 cm of the bone has none: a piece that begins in the top fifth of the bone is the sleeve and hides all above its hem, and a band or a pad below it is another piece, which hides only its own middle, 4 cm from either end. The skin between the two is seen; the first build took the farthest point of the outfit for the hem. On the Site Guard with its forearms taken out and sleeves to 70%, each upper arm keeps 737 and 715 of the body's 1,750 faces and has 1,469 and 1,453 in all, the outfit 18,540, and the gates pass. Seen in the client on that outfit, beside the same outfit worn without its upper-arm part (the body's own arm), standing and in four poses, from six sides each: the copied skin is not told from the body's own, in colour, in shading or at the elbow, where the forearm's maps are twice as fine, and the sleeve's cloth under its roughness map is lit like the chest's under the constant.
- `[repose.py:repose]` - The body is never changed. Each outfit bone is turned onto its body bone, stretched along it to the body's length and scaled across it by the outfit's overall scale; a vertex follows the bones nearest to it, with weights evened over the surface so joints bend smoothly. Faces are kept.
- `[repose.py:fit]` - The outfit is widened only: by a factor evened over neighbouring directions and heights, so relief keeps its shape, then vertex by vertex where it is still inside. Hands and feet are not fitted: gloves and boots replace closed fists and bare toes. Vertex by vertex, a vertex is set outside the least of the body's radii in its own ring and the rings next to it along the bone, not its own ring's alone. The body's radius in a ring is its largest there, and where the body's surface turns across the bone (the top of a shoulder, round the chest's bone) a ring holds the shoulder's full width and the slope above it: on siteguard-male three vertices of the cloth lying on that slope were set 3 to 6 cm out sideways while those beside them stayed, and the faces between stood on the shoulder as spikes once the arm lifted the pad off it.
- `[repose.py:apart]` - Each leg of the fitted outfit stays 2 mm on its own side of the middle between the hip joints. Wide trouser legs fitted round the thighs press into each other, and each lay a few millimetres past the middle: the strip of one leg past it is one surface with the other leg over the crotch and with its own leg below where the cut between the legs ends, so that cut left both thighs one piece and one thigh part got both. A vertex moves back all the way once it follows the legs by half, as the inside of a thigh does, which also follows the pelvis.
- `[split.py:reaches]` - A hip plane's reach is measured on its own leg's side of the middle: the two hip planes tilt apart, and a plane reaching across would leave a band between them that belongs to no part. A torso hanging round the hip joints (a coat) also counts as a curve round them; such an outfit's hip reaches are not to be used.
- `[split.py:assign]` - A joint plane cuts and constrains only within 1.35 joint radii of its joint, measured in the plane; a cut never changes the surface or its winding, and never leaves a new vertex on a whole edge. The joints stand z up and face +y, as the bodies do, or the call fails. A connected piece goes whole to the part most of its area chose, so limbs that touch are not mixed; a piece with two strong rivals is traced again without welding. A piece that still has two then, and holds most of what chose its second part anywhere, is one surface no cut parts and is refused (`[split.py:owners]`): its first part would get both. The legs are also cut apart between the hip joints.
- `[split.py:troughs]` - A fused sleeve is cut out along its arm in the pose the outfit came in, before the bend onto the body (`[repose.py:onto_body]` does it and returns `sleeves` for `split`): with the arms held out the armpit is open, and the seam and the walls are measured on the arm alone. The cut rides through the bend as flags on the vertices it made, and through the joint-plane cuts beside the texture coordinates; `split` parts the pieces along flagged edges and closes them as before. The joint planes still cut at rest, where the body's seams are. Found and cut at rest instead, where the fit presses the sleeve into the torso's side again, the trough took a slab of that side with the sleeve, and the chest's closing under the armpit showed as a ragged panel when the arm swung forward. Without `sleeves`, `split` still finds and cuts the trough on the mesh as it stands.
- `[split.py:pad]` - A shoulder pad on a fused sleeve is cut out along its own outline and goes whole to a shoulder part (`shol`, `shor`), from both sides of the shoulder plane. The shoulder slot's bone is a child of the upper arm's, at the same place, so a shoulder part rides on the arm. - `[brief.py:CONSTRUCTIONS]` - An image prompt says how the outfit is built so that it can be cut into parts: `"construction": 1` in the brief and `{construction}` in its prompt put the paragraph there, and no new reference folder is sealed without the current version (`seal`). - `[brief.py:seal]` - A reference folder is sealed with its brief (`python -m tools.armorkit brief BRIEF --seal FOLDER`): the prompt is written there as `PROMPT.txt` beside a copy of the brief it came from, and nothing is written for a brief that does not hold. `[reference.py:generate]` (`python -m tools.armorkit image BRIEF FOLDER --seed N`) makes the reference image on the local image engine: it seals the folder, sends the sealed prompt and registers each image that comes back. It takes no prompt of its own. An image made from the sealed prompt another way, or derived from a registered image (a clay, a calibrated copy), is registered by what made it (`[brief.py:register]`, `python -m tools.armorkit register FOLDER IMAGE`). Before this the prompt was a hand-written file beside a scratch script, and nothing tied an image to what its outfit is made of.
- `[brief.py:admit]` - Whatever takes an image further, a geometry or an appearance job, first asks whether the image is registered in a sealed folder (`python -m tools.armorkit admit IMAGE --root FOLDER`), and is refused if not. A sealed folder whose prompt was changed by hand lets nothing through. An outfit whose image was made before briefs existed is taken in as it is (`[brief.py:adopt]`, `--adopt FOLDER`): its prompt must be the brief's word for word and its colours as shown must be on record, and they may be closer than a new prompt's may. The jobs that take an image further ask it themselves (`[generate.py:geometry]`).
- `[generate.py:geometry]` - Source jobs on the local engine are posted by the toolkit: `python -m tools.armorkit generate calibrate|geometry|appearance|export WORK MODEL SOURCE --models DIR`. A calibrate, geometry or appearance job is refused before anything is posted unless its image is admitted, and calibrate registers the copies it makes, which are what the later jobs are given. A model gets five geometry attempts, earlier ones included, counted in `trial-ledger.json` in the work folder before the job is posted; an attempt whose outcome is not known stays `submitted` and blocks the next until it is reconciled by hand. Export and appearance are not counted. A busy engine is left alone and nothing is counted. The ledger is locked from before it is read until the attempt's outcome is written (`tools/headkit/gpu_lock.py`): two attempts started together in one work folder would both pass the count and overwrite each other's rows, a sixth job posted with five on record, so the second is refused while the first is under way. Each save writes the ledger to a file of its own and puts it in place whole (`tools.headkit.common.write`): a write cut short would otherwise leave the ledger empty, every model's history gone and the next attempt unable to read it. A model is named exactly as the work folder lists its folder: `outfit/`, `./outfit` or, on Windows, `Outfit` open the same folder and would each start a history of their own in the ledger, five more attempts under another spelling, so any other spelling is refused before the ledger is read. These rules lived in a scratch script before, where a script written anew could leave them out. The route is still a trial route: it posts straight to the engine on headkit's frozen graphs with their hash-checked loaders, and skips the shared submission ledger and the guard's source and runtime checks, so what it makes is trial evidence. The engine's models folder is a machine path and is given on the command line (`--models`), never written down here.
- `[brief.py:worn]` - After the bake the materials the outfit's colours show are matched to the brief's, all pairings tried and the one with the least colour difference in all taken, so a cast every material shares does not turn one into another. A material takes the kind its brief gives it, and with it that kind's channels (`[texture.py:channels]`). If the next best pairing is within 5 (`MARGIN`) the outfit is refused, and its colours as shown are recorded in the brief (`shown`); a different number of materials than the brief lists is refused too. The brief also names the sides the outfit is designed with a shoulder pad on (`pads`, for `[repose.py:onto_body]`'s `padded`).
- `[texture.py:normal_field]` - The generator's mesh has no consistent winding, so its normals are kept as tensors, which read the same whichever way a face is wound; the fine normal is turned to the side the simplified surface faces. A reading that turns more than 75 degrees from the simplified surface is of some other surface close by and is dropped. Caps and closings, which the split made, stay flat. Red runs along u, green along v, and the first stored row of the map is v = 0: seen in the client, the other green sign shows the faces as facets. The material file leaves `texture0` out, so the tint texture stays the part's colour.
- `[texture.py:relief_shade]` - A texel is compared only with texels of its own chart: charts lie a few texels apart in the texture, and would read as bends at each other's edges. One turn counts for 0.35 at most, and the relief changes a shade by a factor between 0.72 and 1.2. A crease the generated colour already shows darker is not darkened twice: the texel takes the darker of the two. This is painted shading; it does not follow the light.
- `[export.py:added_normals]` - The faces the split added (closings and caps, `made`) are lit as surfaces of their own: they get vertices of their own, those on the edge they grew from repeated, with the normals of the added faces alone. A closing's vertices carry what the split copied from that edge, the outfit's normals too, which stand across the closing and not on it; lit by them, every closing and cap was shaded round like the sleeve it closes and showed as a folded fan where an arm swung away from the torso (on an upper arm the normals stood 128 degrees off the faces). A cap is not creased at its rim (`domed`): it rounds the outfit's surface on past the edge, keeps the edge's vertices and normals, and only its own vertices are lit by its own faces. Creased, the top of a thigh that a crouch turns out of the pelvis was lit as a lid set on the trouser leg. Call it on a part after its layout, before it is written.
- `[export.py:model_text]` - A part model names one texture, its own; normals are written, never left to the compiler, so that normals carried through the split keep the shading continuous across a joint. `[export.py:tint]` stores only the layers of the registry's colour channels, first row at v = 0. `[export.py:compile_models]` never compiles a source in place, first removes a model or texture of the same name left in the output folder, and reads every compiled model back: a source cut short compiles without complaint to a model with no mesh.
- `[seams.py:validate_contract]` - The part nearer the torso lies over the other and the chest lies over everything it touches; every joint is measured on both Human Fit bodies (more SRN bodies may be added); tier offsets grow with the tier; each stored tier number equals what the body rings, the tier offsets and the design bend give.
- `[seams.py:mixes]` - Two parts mix cleanly at a joint when the over part's tier is at least the under part's.
- `[client.py:stop]` / `[client.py:start]` - Only a client this module started is stopped, by process id; a client that is already running blocks the run, and so does a process listing that fails.
- `[client_fixture.py:enter_script]` - Each shot schedules the next one, so the game stays `hold` seconds on every shot even after a stall.
- `[client_fixture.py:build]` - The spec is checked before the output folder is made; the folder must be new; fixture names are at most 11 characters so every resource built from them fits a resource name; two packs may not share a file name, and a pack is never copied over an existing link.
- `[client_fixture.py:build]` - The game install is an explicit argument; no machine path is stored in the package.

## Side Effects
- `[client_fixture.py:build]` - Creates a fresh output folder (user directory, staged resources, module sources) and resolves the pinned Neverwinter utilities through `tools/phenotypes/tool_runtime.py`, which registers the run under `.tmp/tool-dependencies`. Packs are hard-linked into the user directory where the volume allows, and their SHA-256 goes into `fixture.json`.
- `[client.py:run]` - Starts and stops `nwmain.exe` on the fixture's own user directory, writes `settings.tml` there on first use and saves window captures.
- `[__main__.py:command_tables]` / `[__main__.py:command_measure]` - Write only to the output folder given.
